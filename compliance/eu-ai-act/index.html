EU AI Act

The EU AI Act establishes a regulatory framework for AI systems across the European Union. AI coding tools like Claude Code and Cursor are internal productivity tools — they are not classified as high-risk AI systems under Annex III of the Act. However, the Act introduces governance and transparency expectations that apply broadly to organisations using AI, and regulators and auditors increasingly use its framework as a benchmark when assessing AI governance maturity. Svalin helps you build a defensible governance posture that addresses the obligations that do apply to AI tool usage.

In force August 2024 · High-risk obligations apply August 2026

Art. 4 AI Literacy

What it requires

Providers and deployers must ensure that staff working with AI systems have sufficient AI literacy to use them appropriately. This obligation entered into force February 2025 and applies regardless of risk classification.

How Svalin addresses it

Policy documentation, session-level audit trails, and configuration enforcement provide the evidence that your organisation has active governance over AI tool usage — supporting a demonstrable AI literacy programme rather than relying on informal practices.

Art. 50 Transparency for GPAI Model Usage

What it requires

Transparency obligations apply to AI systems interacting with people and to content generated by general-purpose AI models. Organisations using GPAI-based tools have a reasonable expectation to document how those models are used and what data flows through them.

How Svalin addresses it

Session-level capture of LLM interactions — model used, duration, data accessed — gives your organisation a documented record of GPAI model usage by developers on enrolled devices. This supports transparency obligations and provides the evidence base for any regulatory enquiry into how AI is used in your environment.

General Governance Posture — Audit Readiness

What regulators expect

Even where AI Act obligations don't directly apply to specific tools, EU regulators and national supervisory authorities use the AI Act framework as a benchmark for assessing AI governance maturity across organisations. Having no audit trail of AI tool usage is an increasingly uncomfortable position for regulated companies — particularly in FinTech, HealthTech, and other supervised sectors.

How Svalin addresses it

Tamper-evident session logs, policy documentation, configuration integrity monitoring, and exportable evidence packs provide the governance artefacts that support any regulatory conversation — regardless of whether specific obligations formally apply to your AI coding tools. When an auditor asks how your organisation governs AI tool usage, Svalin gives you a concrete, documented answer.
